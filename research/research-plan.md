# GrubShelf Market Validation & Usability Study: Research Plan

## Background & Objectives

* **Context:** GrubShelf is a native iOS app that connects household pantry inventory, shared shopping lists, and food spending tracking into one workflow. The app targets households (families, couples, roommates) who want to waste less food, avoid duplicate purchases, and spend more intentionally on groceries. The app is built and approaching launch readiness, but has not been publicly released.

* **The Big Q:** Identify the key pain points in how people currently manage household groceries (tracking what they have, planning what to buy, and controlling food spending) and evaluate whether GrubShelf's feature set addresses the highest-priority gaps.

* **Key Decisions:**
  1. **Go/no-go on App Store launch** — Is there sufficient unmet need for a combined pantry + shopping + budget tool, or do existing solutions (notes apps, single-purpose list apps, spreadsheets) satisfy most people?
  2. **Feature prioritization for v1** — Which of GrubShelf's capabilities (pantry tracking, shared shopping lists, barcode scanning, expiry alerts, budget/spending insights, household sharing) are most valued vs. nice-to-have vs. unwanted?

## Design Summary

* **Method:** Semi-structured interviews (generative research)
* **Sample Size:** 10 participants (broad grocery shoppers segment)
* **Session Duration:** 30 minutes per interview
* **Timeline:**
  - Recruitment & screening: Days 1-2
  - Interviews: Days 3-7
  - Analysis & synthesis: Days 8-10

## Participant Profile

* **Primary Behaviors:**
  - Buys groceries at least once per week (in-store or delivery)
  - Has thrown away food due to expiry, over-buying, or forgetting what they had in the last 30 days
  - Currently uses at least one method to track groceries (mental list, notes app, paper list, shared list app, spreadsheet — anything counts)
* **Required Context:**
  - Shops for a household of 2+ people, OR shops solo but has experienced duplicate purchases or food waste
* **Exclusion Criteria:**
  - No employees of grocery delivery companies, food tech startups, or market research firms
  - No professional chefs or food industry workers (atypical grocery behavior)

## Analysis Approach

* **Method:** Hybrid coding (deductive start from interview guide topics + inductive in-vivo codes from participant language)
* **Prioritization Framework:** Opportunity Scoring (Importance vs. Satisfaction) — because we're evaluating market gaps
* **Synthesis:** Thematic analysis with behavioral persona construction
* **Deliverable:** Research report with themes, opportunity scores, personas, quotes, and feature prioritization recommendations
