# GrubShelf Market Validation & Usability Study: Interview Guide

## Research Context

* **The Big Q:** Identify the key pain points in how people currently manage household groceries and evaluate whether GrubShelf's feature set addresses the highest-priority gaps.
* **Key Decisions:** Go/no-go on launch; which features to prioritize for v1.
* **The Little q:** "Tell me about how you and your household handle food — from figuring out what to buy, to shopping, to what happens once the groceries are home. Start wherever feels natural."

## Interviewer Reminders

> - You are the apprentice; the participant is the master. Learn from them.
> - After the opening prompt, wait 60 seconds before speaking again.
> - When you hear something interesting, try "Oh?" before a longer probe.
> - Never correct the participant. A "wrong" answer is a finding, not a mistake.
> - Mirror their words. Don't introduce jargon they haven't used.
> - Push for **specific incidents**, not generalizations. "I usually..." → "Tell me about a specific time."

## Session Outline (30 Minutes)

### 1. Introduction & Orientation (3 mins)

- **Script:** "Thanks for taking the time to talk with me. I'm researching how people manage their groceries and household food — what works, what doesn't, and what could be better. There are no right or wrong answers. I'm genuinely interested in your real experience, not a polished version. I'll be recording so I can focus on our conversation. Is that OK?"
- Get verbal recording consent.

### 2. Warm-up & Context Setting (5 mins)

- **Icebreaker:** "First, tell me a bit about your household — who lives there, and who does most of the grocery shopping?"
- **The Little q:** "Now walk me through how you and your household handle food — from figuring out what to buy, to the actual shopping, to what happens once the groceries come home. Start wherever feels natural."
- *After asking, wait. Let them fill the silence. Follow up on anything specific or emotional.*

### 3. Core Topics & Story Excavation (18 mins)

#### Topic 1: Knowing What You Have (Pantry Awareness)

- **Main Prompt:** "Tell me about a recent time you discovered food had gone bad or expired in your kitchen. What happened?"
- **Follow-up Probes:**
  - "How did you find out? Were you looking for it, or did you stumble on it?"
  - "How often does that kind of thing happen?"
  - "Do you currently have any way of keeping track of what food you have at home?"
  - "What would need to change for that to happen less?"
- *If participant generalizes ("I usually just check the fridge"), redirect: "Can you tell me about a specific time this week?"*

#### Topic 2: Shopping Planning & Coordination

- **Main Prompt:** "Think about the last time you went grocery shopping. How did you decide what to buy?"
- **Follow-up Probes:**
  - "Did you use a list? How did you make it?"
  - "Has there been a time when you bought something and got home to find you already had it?"
  - "If you shop with or for other people, how do you coordinate? What goes wrong?"
  - "What tools or apps, if any, do you use for shopping lists?"
- *Note: Listen for shared-list pain, duplicate-buy stories, and coordination friction.*

#### Topic 3: Food Spending & Budget

- **Main Prompt:** "How do you feel about what your household spends on food each month?"
- **Follow-up Probes:**
  - "Do you track grocery spending? If so, how?"
  - "Tell me about a time you felt you spent too much. What happened?"
  - "Do you have a sense of how much food you waste in terms of money?"
  - "If you could see exactly where your food money goes, what would you do with that information?"

#### Topic 4: Current Tools & Workarounds

- **Main Prompt:** "What tools, apps, or habits do you rely on right now to manage groceries?"
- **Follow-up Probes:**
  - "What works well about your current approach?"
  - "What's the most frustrating part?"
  - "Have you tried any grocery or pantry apps before? What happened?"
  - "What would your ideal solution look like?"
- *Note: Pay attention to whether they describe wanting a single integrated tool or prefer separate tools.*

### 4. Concept Reaction — GrubShelf (Optional, if time allows)

- **Setup:** "I'm going to briefly describe an app concept. I'd like your honest reaction — there are no wrong answers, and critical feedback is just as valuable as positive."
- **Concept Description:** "Imagine an app where your household shares a pantry inventory — you can scan barcodes or search to add items, and it tracks what you have, what's running low, and what's expiring. Shopping lists are shared too, so anyone in the household can add items, and when you finish shopping, you transfer those items into your pantry. It also tracks what you spend on groceries against a budget you set."
- **Probes:**
  - "What's your first reaction?"
  - "Which part of that sounds most useful to you? Least useful?"
  - "What would make you actually use something like this vs. abandoning it after a week?"
  - "What's missing from what I described?"

### 5. Wrap-up (4 mins)

- **Summary Check:** "From what I've heard, it sounds like [summarize 2-3 key themes]. Did I get that right? Anything I missed?"
- **Magic Wand:** "If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about how your household handles food and groceries, what would it be?"
- **Catch-all:** "Is there anything about managing food at home that I should have asked about but didn't?"
- **Referral:** "Do you know anyone else who deals with grocery management challenges who might be willing to talk?"
- **Closing:** "Thank you — your input is genuinely helpful. [Confirm incentive details.]"
